## About Me

Hello! I am **Riya Basak**, a BSc (Hons) Computer Science with Artificial Intelligence graduate from the University of Hertfordshire, preparing for **integrated MS–PhD study and research in artificial intelligence**.

My research focus is **world models for general, reliable intelligence**: learning internal representations that capture latent state, transferable dynamics, causal structure, uncertainty, and the consequences of actions. I am interested in systems that can transfer what they learn to new environments, reason over imagined futures, recognise when their predictions are unreliable, and choose actions under explicit safety constraints.

My completed work has examined **shortcut learning in medical vision** and **admissibility-constrained offline reinforcement learning**. My current direction is toward **self-supervised predictive world models, compositional and causal generalisation, counterfactual safety, and embodied decision-making**.

## Research Interests

- **World Models & Transferable Dynamics** — structured latent state, causal and compositional dynamics, long-horizon prediction, and transfer across changes in context, appearance, and embodiment.
- **Self-Supervised Predictive Representation Learning** — representations that preserve task-relevant structure and dynamics without depending on dense supervision or surface shortcuts.
- **Safe Sequential Decision-Making** — model-based reasoning, uncertainty-aware planning, offline reinforcement learning, admissibility constraints, and counterfactual safety analysis.
- **Embodied AI** — agents that use learned world dynamics to anticipate physical and social consequences before acting.

## Research Direction & Impact

My long-term goal is to develop AI systems that can **learn reusable models of how the world changes**, **separate causal mechanisms from nuisance variation**, **simulate plausible futures before acting**, and **remain controllable when predictions are uncertain or candidate actions are unsafe**. The research objective is not benchmark performance alone, but reliable generalisation and decision-making when environments, tasks, or embodiments change.
